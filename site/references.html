References

What each generative system in vizz is, and where it comes from. Most of these are older than the app by decades; a few are ours, and this page says which.

A source here is the idea, not the implementation. Every system below is written out in this repository rather than pulled in as a library, so where a paper and the code disagree the entry says how. Where there is no outside source, the line says vizz rather than leaving a blank that reads like a missing citation.

Geometry

Lorenz

The butterfly. Three coupled differential equations whose trajectory never repeats and never leaves a bounded region. vizz integrates it once on the CPU at startup with σ 10, ρ 28, β 8/3 — the classic values — discards the first 20,000 steps as transient, and stores the next 65,536 points in trajectory order. Consecutive points are consecutive in time, so advancing every particle's index makes the cloud crawl along the attractor rather than shimmer chaotically in place.

Source: Edward N. Lorenz, “Deterministic Nonperiodic Flow”, Journal of the Atmospheric Sciences 20(2), 1963.

Aizawa

Rounder and shell-like, with a spike through the poles — chosen as a contrast partner for Lorenz, because the morph between the two has somewhere to go. Integrated and stored the same way.

Source: named for Yoji Aizawa, whose work on chaotic flows it comes out of. Unlike Lorenz it has no single canonical paper behind it: the parameter set vizz uses is the one that circulates in graphics, and Analog Paradigm's write-up states the same equations.

Trefoil knot

The simplest non-trivial knot, drawn as a core curve and thickened into a tube by scattering particles around it. vizz uses the standard (2, 3) torus-knot parametrisation.

Source: classical knot theory; the parametrisation is the standard one, as given on MathWorld.

Solid sphere and hollow shell

Both are sampled uniformly by volume rather than by angle, which is the difference between a sphere and a sphere with a bright clot at each pole. Height is drawn uniformly from −1 to 1 and the ring radius follows from it; for the solid sphere the radius is scaled by the cube root of a uniform draw, and for the shell it stays near the surface.

Source: the uniform-height trick is Archimedes' hat-box theorem — equal slices of a sphere carry equal area — in the form usually written up as sphere point picking.

Torus, grid plane, cloud pair

A torus from its two angles, a grid plane with a travelling ripple, and the cloud pair, which is not a shape at all but a blend between any two loaded clouds — a scan, an attractor, a word, a live video frame.

Source: vizz. Plain parametric forms with nothing behind them worth citing.

Pattern layers

The stack

The print-look counterpart to the particle field: eight procedural pattern layers — rings, stripes, checker, polygon, star, rays, dots — in flat ink colours on coloured paper, each with its own similarity transform, drawn in a single fullscreen pass and composited in-register.

Source: 555-5555's visuals for Caribou's live shows, which is the look this was built to reach. Not an algorithm and not a paper — an idiom, borrowed knowingly. The generators themselves are ordinary procedural patterns.

Moiré

The reason the stack has eight layers and not one. Two periodic patterns at close frequencies interfere into a third, much coarser pattern that neither layer contains, and it moves far faster than either layer does when you drift one of them. The built-in show is largely made of this.

Source: Isaac Amidror, The Theory of the Moiré Phenomenon, Volume I: Periodic Layers, Springer.

Blend modes

Multiply, screen, add, difference, exclusion and subtract, the vocabulary the look is built in.

Source: the formulas are the separable blend modes of W3C Compositing and Blending Level 1. vizz departs from the spec in one deliberate way: it blends on sRGB-encoded values rather than linear ones, because compositing on encoded values is the print-era behaviour this aesthetic grew out of. The single conversion to linear happens on the way out of the shader.

Analytic antialiasing

Hard-edged patterns alias badly, and the usual fix — fwidth — lies at exactly the discontinuities these patterns are made of. Instead each layer carries one scalar for how many pattern units a pixel spans, propagated by the chain rule through the layer transform, and each generator turns a signed distance into coverage over that footprint. Past Nyquist the coverage converges to flat duty-cycle tone, so the moiré survives where the sparkle does not.

Source: the technique is the one Iñigo Quilez describes in “filtering procedural textures”. The per-generator gradient magnitudes are worked out for these generators specifically.

Colour and tone

Cosine palettes

The four built-in gradients are colour = a + b·cos(τ(c·t + d)), four coefficients each. They are baked into a lookup texture on the CPU rather than evaluated in the shader, so a palette you load from a list of colour stops sits in the same bank and is indistinguishable from a built-in as far as the renderer is concerned.

Source: Iñigo Quilez, “palettes”.

HSV

Palette 0 stays procedural rather than baked, so /particles/hue keeps meaning a rotation through hue rather than an offset into a ramp.

Source: Alvy Ray Smith, “Color Gamut Transform Pairs”, SIGGRAPH 1978 — the paper HSV comes from.

The tone shoulder

Glow and trails push values well past 1, and a hard clip turns highlights into flat white blobs. The composite pass ends with c / (1 + 0.15c), which rolls the top off and leaves midtones essentially untouched.

Source: the c/(1+c) curve from Reinhard, Stark, Shirley and Ferwerda, “Photographic Tone Reproduction for Digital Images”, SIGGRAPH 2002. The 0.15 is tuned for this picture, not from the paper.

sRGB

The transfer function the vector stack encodes and decodes with, and the one the output textures are in.

Source: IEC 61966-2-1.

Light and point clouds

Estimated normals

Most scanners export points with no surface direction, and the sun cannot light a surface it cannot orient. vizz reads normals from a PLY that carries them and works them out from each point's twelve nearest neighbours when it does not: fit a plane through the neighbourhood, and the plane's normal is the surface's — the eigenvector of the neighbourhood's covariance with the smallest eigenvalue.

Source: Hoppe, DeRose, Duchamp, McDonald and Stuetzle, “Surface Reconstruction from Unorganized Points”, SIGGRAPH 1992, where the local plane fit is step one. vizz stops there: the paper goes on to propagate a consistent orientation across the cloud, and vizz instead flips each normal towards the eye, which is a better answer for a scan of a room seen from inside it.

Lamps and the sun

Two movable lamps that fall off with distance and work on anything with a position, and a directional sun that lights surfaces — the wall facing it comes up, the wall facing away goes down. A cloud with no normals is left alone by the sun rather than lit from a direction nobody measured.

Source: Lambert's cosine law (Johann Heinrich Lambert, Photometria, 1760) for the directional term. The distance falloff is a smooth r²/(d²+r²) rather than inverse-square, so there is no singularity to divide by on stage.

PLY

The format a scan arrives in, ASCII or binary little-endian, and also the wire format for live point-cloud streaming — there is no wrapper protocol, because a PLY header already states its own frame length.

Source: the Stanford polygon format, Greg Turk, Stanford University.

Depth of field

Done by resizing the sprite rather than blurring the frame: a defocused point light is a larger, dimmer disc, so the particle's billboard grows with its distance from the focus plane and dims by the square of that growth. Closer to the real thing than a post-process blur, and it costs nothing.

Source: vizz, on the ordinary optics of the circle of confusion.

Randomness

The particle hash

Every particle is derived from its index and nothing else — no state carried between frames, which is what makes the count free to change mid-set and the whole field scrubbable. Four independent random streams per particle come out of one integer mixer. It replaced a float hash whose repeats at high counts stacked particles on each other and pushed them into the tone-map shoulder; an integer mixer has no such cliff, and is stable across GPUs in a way anything built on sin() is not.

Source: the lowbias32 constants from Chris Wellons' hash prospector, described in “Prospecting for Hash Functions”.

Audio

Window and spectrum

A 2048-point FFT at 48 kHz — a 43 ms window, 23 Hz a bin — through a periodic Hann window, with the band magnitudes corrected for the window's power loss so that a band meter and a gain control mean something absolute rather than something relative to the window.

Source: the transform itself is the rustfft crate; the window and its correction factor are from Fredric J. Harris, “On the Use of Windows for Harmonic Analysis with the Discrete Fourier Transform”, Proceedings of the IEEE 66(1), 1978, and Parseval's theorem for the normalisation.

Onset detection

Positive spectral flux: how much energy appeared since the last frame, summing only the bins that went up. Summing the drops as well would make a note ending look like a note starting.

Source: the half-wave-rectified spectral flux detector, surveyed and compared in Simon Dixon, “Onset Detection Revisited”, DAFx-06.

Tempo

Autocorrelation of the onset signal: if the music has a pulse, the onset signal correlates with itself at the beat period, and peak-picking the correlation gives a BPM without anything ever having to decide what a beat is. The characteristic failure is the octave error — 180 for a 90 BPM track — because a signal that correlates at one period also correlates at its multiples. Two guards: a prior preferring the middle of the range, and a check for whether half the candidate explains the signal comparably well. A confidence figure comes out alongside, so ambient material with no pulse reports low confidence rather than a confident wrong answer.

Source: autocorrelation of an onset-strength envelope, as set out in Daniel P. W. Ellis, “Beat Tracking by Dynamic Programming”, Journal of New Music Research 36(1), 2007. vizz uses the tempo-estimation half and not the dynamic-programming beat tracker — a VJ needs the rate, and the phase comes from the pads.

The frame

Feedback trails

The pass that makes the output look like VJ material. Last frame's result is zoomed and rotated a little and mixed back in with the new one, so motion smears into trails and a sustained zoom builds a tunnel out of whatever is on screen. A lerp rather than an accumulation, because adding the history outright is a geometric series that saturates to white within a second.

Source: analogue video feedback, which is where this whole idiom comes from — Steina and Woody Vasulka's work and the Sandin Image Processor (Dan Sandin, 1971–73). The implementation is an ordinary ping-pong of two Rgba16Float textures.

Kaleidoscope and lens

Mirror, quad mirror and a six-wedge kaleidoscope, all done as a fold of UV space before sampling; a radial RGB split that leaves green alone, so the frame fringes towards the edges the way a lens does instead of shifting hue; and a cheap six-tap bloom.

Source: vizz. Standard screen-space constructions with no particular paper behind them.

Gravity wells

Four attractors and repulsors bending the cloud from a layer above the scenes. Deliberately not a simulation: every particle here is a function of its index with no state between frames, and integrating velocities would throw that away for physics nobody is checking. The falloff is r²/(d²+r²) — one at the centre, a half at the radius, asymptotically nothing beyond — because a hard cutoff shows up as a visible shell in the cloud.

Source: vizz. A displacement field that reads as gravity, not a gravity model.

Camera moves

Ten paths — orbit, sway, push, pull, crane, spiral, look around, fly through, walkthrough and a slow drift — each returning a delta added to the camera the faders already describe, with rate in bars so a move locks to the same clock as the sequencer.

Source: vizz. The shapes are borrowed from camera work rather than from anything written down.
